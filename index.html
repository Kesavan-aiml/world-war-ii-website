Chapter 01 · The Road to War

How did World War II start?

World War II grew from several connected problems. The aftermath of World War I, the Great Depression, aggressive dictatorships, expansionism and weak international responses created a dangerous situation.

01 · AFTER WORLD WAR I

Treaty of Versailles

Germany lost territory, faced military restrictions and reparations. Anger over the settlement was later exploited by Adolf Hitler and the Nazi movement.

02 · ECONOMIC CRISIS

The Great Depression

Worldwide economic hardship caused unemployment and political instability, helping extremist movements gain support.

03 · DICTATORSHIPS

Fascism & Nazism

Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy promoted authoritarian rule, militarism and territorial expansion. Japan's militarist leadership also expanded across Asia.

04 · EXPANSION

Aggressive Conquests

Japan expanded in East Asia, Italy invaded Ethiopia, and Germany remilitarized and expanded into Austria and Czechoslovakia.

05 · DIPLOMACY

Appeasement

Britain and France tried to avoid another European war by making concessions to Germany, most famously at Munich in 1938.

06 · SEPTEMBER 1939

Invasion of Poland

Germany invaded Poland on September 1, 1939. Britain and France declared war on Germany on September 3.

Immediate trigger: Germany's invasion of Poland on September 1, 1939.
Chapter 02 · The Global War

Major events timeline

Follow the conflict from its beginning in Europe to its end in Asia.

1939

Poland is invaded

Germany invades Poland. Britain and France declare war; the Soviet Union occupies eastern Poland later that month.

1940

France falls · Battle of Britain

Germany defeats France. Britain remains in the war after resisting Germany's air campaign.

1941

Germany invades the Soviet Union

Operation Barbarossa opens the Eastern Front and greatly expands the scale of the war.

DECEMBER 7, 1941

Pearl Harbor

Japan attacks Pearl Harbor. The United States enters the war against Japan and soon against Germany and Italy.

1942–1943

Turning points

Allied victories at Midway and El Alamein, together with the Soviet victory at Stalingrad, shift momentum against the Axis powers.

JUNE 6, 1944

D-Day

Allied forces land in Normandy and establish a major Western Front against Nazi Germany.

MAY 1945

Germany surrenders

Berlin falls. Hitler dies on April 30. Germany's unconditional surrender takes effect in May.

AUGUST–SEPTEMBER 1945

Japan surrenders

Atomic bombs are used against Hiroshima and Nagasaki, the Soviet Union enters the war against Japan, and Japan formally signs its surrender on September 2.

Chapter 04 · Battles & Turning Points

Events that changed the war

1940 · EUROPE

Battle of Britain

Germany attempted to gain air superiority over Britain. The campaign failed to force Britain out of the war.

1941 · EASTERN FRONT

Operation Barbarossa

Germany invaded the Soviet Union in the largest land invasion of its time, opening a brutal Eastern Front.

1942 · PACIFIC

Battle of Midway

A major U.S. naval victory that severely weakened Japan's carrier strength and shifted momentum in the Pacific.

1942–43 · EASTERN FRONT

Stalingrad

The Soviet victory became a major turning point against Nazi Germany and inflicted enormous losses on German forces.

1942–43 · NORTH AFRICA

El Alamein

British and Allied forces defeated Axis forces in Egypt, helping secure the Suez Canal route.

JUNE 1944 · FRANCE

D-Day & Normandy

More than 150,000 Allied troops landed in Normandy on D-Day, opening the path toward the liberation of Western Europe.

Chapter 05 · Human Tragedy

The Holocaust

The Holocaust was the systematic persecution and murder of six million Jews by Nazi Germany and its collaborators. Millions of other victims were also persecuted and murdered, including Roma, disabled people, Soviet prisoners of war, Polish civilians and political opponents.

Understanding the Holocaust is essential to understanding the human consequences of Nazi ideology, racism and dictatorship.

Chapter 06 · The Atomic Age

The atomic bombs

The final weeks of the Pacific War introduced nuclear weapons to warfare. The bombings of Hiroshima and Nagasaki remain among the most consequential and debated events of the 20th century.

MANHATTAN PROJECT

How were the bombs developed?

The United States led the secret Manhattan Project, with support from Britain and Canada, to develop nuclear weapons during World War II. Scientists worked at several sites, including Los Alamos in New Mexico.

The first nuclear test, Trinity, took place on July 16, 1945.

AUGUST 6, 1945

Hiroshima — “Little Boy”

The United States dropped an atomic bomb called Little Boy on Hiroshima. The city suffered catastrophic destruction and loss of life; many people also died later from injuries and radiation effects.

AUGUST 9, 1945

Nagasaki — “Fat Man”

Three days later, the United States dropped Fat Man on Nagasaki, causing enormous destruction and thousands of deaths.

WHY IT MATTERS

Japan's surrender

Japan announced its acceptance of surrender terms on August 15, 1945. The Soviet Union had also entered the war against Japan. The formal surrender was signed on September 2.

Chapter 08 · The End

How did World War II end?

APRIL 1945

Fall of Berlin

Soviet forces captured Berlin as Nazi Germany's military position collapsed.

MAY 8, 1945

Victory in Europe

Germany's unconditional surrender took effect. The war in Europe was over.

AUGUST 1945

Japan under pressure

Hiroshima and Nagasaki were bombed while the Soviet Union entered the war against Japan.

AUGUST 15, 1945

Japan accepts surrender

Emperor Hirohito announced Japan's decision to accept Allied surrender terms.

SEPTEMBER 2, 1945

Formal surrender

Japan signed surrender documents aboard USS Missouri, formally ending World War II.

Chapter 09 · A New World

What happened after the war?

The end of fighting created another enormous challenge: rebuilding societies, responding to war crimes and creating institutions for a more stable international order.

United Nations

The UN was established in 1945 to promote international cooperation and help maintain peace and security.

Nuremberg Trials

Senior Nazi officials were prosecuted for crimes including war crimes and crimes against humanity.

Marshall Plan

The United States launched a major European recovery program in 1948, helping rebuild economies and infrastructure.

Occupation & Reconstruction

Germany and Japan were occupied and underwent major political, economic and social reforms.

Cold War

Relations between the United States and Soviet Union deteriorated, producing decades of geopolitical rivalry.

Decolonization

Anti-colonial movements grew stronger as European powers emerged weakened from the war, contributing to independence across Asia and Africa.
